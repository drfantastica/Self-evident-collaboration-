Dear Professor Zhao,

I'm writing regarding your group's recent work on shape and multiple shape coexistence using the five-dimensional collective Hamiltonian based on PC-PK1 (Phys. Rev. C **107**, 024308 (2023)). The systematic coverage of even-even nuclei from 10 ≤ Z ≤ 104 is, as far as I can tell, the most comprehensive 5DCH dataset published to date — congratulations on a remarkable piece of work.

I'm currently developing a methodology paper that examines the Thouless-Valatin / collective inertia tensor M_ij(β, γ) from a topological perspective. The starting observation is that the inertia tensor entering the 5DCH is structurally identical to a Berry curvature on the Bohr-Mottelson shape manifold — its time-odd contributions are exactly ⟨∂_i Ψ | ∂_j Ψ⟩ in disguise, which means the inertia tensor carries gauge-invariant topological information that, to my knowledge, has not been systematically extracted.

The specific question I'd like to ask: do the inertia tensors M_ij(β, γ) computed in your paper carry integer-quantized Chern-number structure when integrated over surfaces enclosing shape-phase-transition critical points? My framework predicts integer winding numbers at X(5)-type and E(5)-type critical points; testing this requires the underlying tabulated M_ij(β, γ) data, which doesn't appear to be in the supplementary material of the paper itself.

**Concrete ask:** would it be possible to access the tabulated inertia tensor data — M_ij(β, γ) on the (β, γ) grids you used — for a representative subset of nuclei spanning shape phase transitions? The most useful regions for this test would be the rare-earth A ≈ 150 isotones (Sm-Nd, X(5) transition) and the Ru-Pd region (E(5) transition). If a global subset is too large to share directly, even five to ten well-chosen nuclei across these transitions would be sufficient to test the prediction.

I'd be glad to share the full methodology in advance of any data exchange so you can evaluate whether the question is well-posed, and naturally any results would acknowledge your dataset prominently — with co-authorship if the result holds and you'd like to be involved in writing it up.

Happy to do a video call if it would be easier to discuss the framing.
